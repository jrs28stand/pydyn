# OBC news methods: performance and reliability (pydyn)

Protocol: `BENCH_PROTOCOL.md` (docs/OBC_BENCH_PROTOCOL.md). Branch `obc-perf` (backed up as `wip/obc-perf`). Harness: `bench/obc_bench.py`;
raw results `bench/results/*.json`; tables are generated by `bench/make_perf_tables.py` and `bench/obc_report.py`.

## How it was measured
- "Before" is `bench/obc_before.py`, a frozen copy of `pydyn/obc.py` at commit cd909fa. "After" is the current `pydyn.obc`. Both run in the same
  process, interleaved, 5 repeats; min and median reported. OccBin is the same code in both columns. Seed 20261124. Machine: 2 cores shared with
  other workers, run under `nice`; medians are noisy (load average 1-3), minima are the more reliable column. The P1 ratios between runs vary by
  about 10-20%.
- Models: zlb, zlb2, nkm (Dynare tests/occbin/filter/NKM_0_std_shocks.mod), sw (reference/obc_sw_zlb.mod). Horizon 40, check-ahead 40 for the news
  methods; OccBin check-ahead 80.
- Shock scale s (draws are N(0,1) times the shock standard deviation, 0.01 where zero, times s): zlb 2, zlb2 2, nkm 4 (binding shares 36%, 37%, 41%).
  **sw cannot meet the 30-60% rule**: with the base-line a -4 `eb` shock it binds, but under random shocks OccBin and the general formulation both fail
  somewhere in 1,000 periods for s >= 0.29 (see `bench/results/calibrate_sw.json`; the scan was s = 0.02..12 and 0.22..0.5). s = 0.25 is the largest
  scale that runs the full 1,000 periods: binding share 7.5%. (s = 0.28 also ran, share 10.3%, but 0.27 and 0.29 failed, so 0.25 is the safe choice.)
- "RegimeOBC" is called `regime` below, the LCP formulation `news`, LKNews (main session, `pydyn/obc_lk.py`) `lk`. `news` is not applicable to sw
  (multi_equation); on nkm the LCP is the approximate class, so its paths differ from OccBin's by up to 1.3e-4 (regime histories differ too) in P1;
  this is a property of the formulation (contract section 3.6), not of the speed work: after-vs-before differences are 3e-13 with identical histories.
- Correctness in every table: path difference against OccBin and against the before code; regime histories equal or not.

## Changes and what each bought

1. **No full path per period (next state only; full path once after the last shock).** `NewsOBC._step`, `RegimeOBC._step`. Profile of nkm news,
   1,000 periods, before: 0.583 s total, `base_path` 0.358 s (61%, 1,000 calls), `solve_period` own time 0.037 s, active set 0.101 s cumulative.
   After this change alone (stepA run, `p1_stepA.json`): news per period 470 -> 95 us on nkm, 294 -> 62 on zlb, 473 -> 100 on zlb2, regime on sw 467 -> 103.
2. **q and the next state as maps from the state variables only.** `_prepare_step`: columns with norm below 1e-14 of the scale are dropped (zlb: 1 of 5
   variables is a state; zlb2: 1 of 7; nkm: 7 of 24; sw: 12 of 32). The dropped columns are numerically zero (tested). `RegimeOBC` also keeps only the
   paths of the variables its conditions read (`_cv`), so the check of a regime guess works on a small array. The gain is in the per-period matrix-vector products
   (80 x 32 -> 80 x 7 on nkm) and, for RegimeOBC, in the check; not separable from change 1 in the measurements.
3. **Warm start** (previous binding set shifted by one period), `simulate(..., warm_start=True)`, off by default. Measured (`warm_D.json`, min us/period cold -> warm):
   zlb news 26.1 -> 32.7, zlb2 36.2 -> 41.7, nkm 34.0 -> 38.7; regime zlb 46.6 -> 52.4, zlb2 68.0 -> 83.2, nkm 57.6 -> 63.1, sw 34.9 -> 39.0.
   **Slower everywhere**, identical results. Reason: with a fresh shock every period the shifted set is rarely the answer (a prior offline count on zlb:
   247 of 355 binding periods accepted the warm guess but it needed 607 solves against 355 cold). It would pay only with few or no new shocks.
4. **O(T) build of M and the wedge responses.** `_functional_rows`, `_wedge_vectors`, `_response_matrix`: with rows r_t of the functional (r_0 = aB + aA P,
   r_{t+1} = r_t P) and v_s = G^s F e, each diagonal of M obeys M[t, s+1] = M[t-1, s] + r_t v_{s+1}; one matrix product gives all r_t v_s and the rest
   is vector additions. A single LU of (B0 + A0 P) replaces the explicit inverse. The (N+1, n, nT) array `Yw` is built only on first use (`Yw` property)
   and the path after wedges is a backward-forward recursion (`_wedge_path`). Verified: M against `stacked_M` to 1e-10 (new test, horizon 12 and padded
   stack), M against the old build to 3e-16 (zlb2), 1.6e-15 (nkm); Yw to 6e-14. Set-up profile nkm news before: `_build` 0.033 s of 0.040 s (tensordot 0.016);
   after: `_build` 6 ms of 9 ms in the same measurement, 2.0 ms in P2 (see tables); the remainder is cProfile overhead plus `lstsq` fits in the post-processor.
   Scaling with horizon (P4): before 6.5 / 12.2 / 25.5 ms at T = 20/40/80 on nkm news, after 1.9 / 2.0 / 2.4 ms; sw regime 7.2 / 15.8 / 79.6 -> 2.1 / 2.1 / 4.2 ms.
   The construction is not strictly O(T) overall: the product r_t V is O(N n T) and the diagonal sweep O(N T) vector adds, with n fixed; the remaining T growth is
   visible at 80 on sw (4.2 ms).
5. **Parameter-only objects cached.** `constraint_objects` result, `_affine_condition` and the compiled condition functions are kept on the Occbin instance
   (which is built per parameter set) or on the model (compiled code), and the finite-difference gradients only loop over the variables a condition names.
   Identical `ConstraintObject`s (checked field by field on all four models). `constraint_objects(ob)`: nkm 0.85 -> 0.31 ms on a fresh instance, 0.0006 ms on a repeated call.
   Not done: avoiding checks of all rows; I found nothing safe to drop (the check-ahead rows are needed for the horizon_too_short verdict).
6. **Other profile-driven changes.** Active-set loop touches only the T complementarity rows and uses LAPACK `dgesv` with integer indexing (np.linalg.solve 4.9 us vs
   dgesv 1.5 us on 8x8; `np.ix_` 3.4 vs 0.9 us); the full-row check only once in the final verification. Regime histories built vectorised (`_map_regime`). Unconstrained
   path recursion on the state variables only (`_linear_path`).

## Lessons for the Julia port
- Compute the next state only; the full path is needed once (last shock period). This was the largest single gain (4-5x) and is trivial to port.
- Make q (and the regime formulation's alternative residual) a map from the *state* variables and shocks. Detect states from the columns of P and of the q-map, not from the model declaration.
- Never form M, Yw as loops over (t, s) with n x n products. One matrix product r_t V (rows of the functional against the news vectors G^s F e) plus the diagonal
  recursion M[t, s+1] = M[t-1, s] + r_t v_{s+1} gives M in a few ms; use one factorisation of (B0 + A0 P) instead of an inverse. Build the (N+1, n, nT) response array only if something asks for it.
- Cache everything that depends on parameters only: post-processor results, compiled condition functions, finite-difference gradients restricted to the variables named in a condition.
- Per-period cost in an interpreted language is dominated by call overhead, not flops (8x8 solve 1.5 us via LAPACK vs 4.9 us through numpy's wrapper). In Julia the equivalent is avoiding allocations; keep a pre-allocated buffer per spell size.
- Warm-starting from the previous binding set did not pay with a fresh shock each period (slower in all models); do not port it as default.
- Time everything before and after in the same process; ratios between runs on a loaded machine move by 10-20%.
- R1 on sw: an equal failure set between OccBin and the general formulation is expected (no solution exists); do not budget time to "fix" it; budget a time limit on integer programmes (0.5 s each cost 80-140 s per 2,000 states).
- The approximate class (nkm) makes the LCP and OccBin differ by up to 1.3e-4 in paths over 1,000 periods; compare against OccBin with that tolerance or with the RegimeOBC formulation.

## Not measured / open
- A compiled kernel (numba/Julia-like) for the active-set loop: not added (no new dependencies). Estimate: per binding period the work is a handful of 40 x 40 matvecs and an 8 x 8 solve, about 5-10 us of arithmetic against 40-60 us measured, so a compiled loop would gain roughly 4-6x on binding periods and 2-3x on slack periods (9 us, mostly Python overhead). Not measured.
- sw at the 30-60% binding share: not achievable without failures; P1/P3 for sw use s = 0.25 (7.5% binding), so its per-period numbers are mostly the slack-period cost.
- RegimeOBC integer programme (the fallback) was not sped up; two constraints take minutes. Formulation unchanged by instruction.
- `solve_news` (the integer programme of the LCP) was never needed in P1 or R1 on the benchmark models (the active set solved everything); its speed is unmeasured except the old figures in OBC_PROGRESS.md.
- Estimation-type P3 for LKNews: its per-spell cache would be rebuilt per parameter set; the first-use cost was not included in P2.
- Medians are noisy (shared machine); the before/after columns come from the same process but the load changed between sections, so compare ratios inside a table only.
- The P3 perturbation (1%) was chosen so that nkm does not fail; other parameters of nkm fail often (48 of 50 for beta, thetap), for OccBin and both news methods alike.
- sw_timeout_check.py (re-run of time-outs at 30 s) not completed.
- Not verified: behaviour with check_ahead values other than 40, or horizons above 80, after the merge.

## Remaining speed ideas (estimates, not measured)
1. Compiled active-set/LCP kernel: 4-6x on binding periods (see above).
2. Per-period overhead in Python (argument checks, `np.concatenate`, `verify_lcp` over 80 rows): batching several independent states (estimation across particles or draws) through one matrix-matrix product, 3-5x for slack periods.
3. Reuse the factorisation across the two parameters perturbed in estimation (first-order solve 0.3-0.7 ms and post-processor 0.3 ms are now a third of P2, 2 ms): analytic derivatives of the affine conditions instead of finite differences, 10-20% of P2.
